01 · THE EXPERIENCE

Why I Took Part

On October 4, 2026, I volunteered at a TechnoCraft STEM workshop at Yatana Bon Yeik Nyein Monastic Education Center. The students were around 14–16 years old, and the workshop was designed to give them an introduction to computing and electronics through things they could actually try themselves.

One thing that stayed with me was how unfamiliar technology was for most of the students. They did not have computer science classes at school, and some of them are not allowed to use smartphones or laptops. When we talked about what they wanted to do in the future, many naturally mentioned jobs they already saw around them like bus driver and football player. They thought working in STEM is something that is far away from their reach.

So the point of the workshop wasn't just to teach them a few coding commands. We wanted them to see that computers, robots and electronics are not mysterious things that only other people can understand. They can learn the logic behind them and build things themselves too.

02 · MY ROLE

What I Actually Did

I had two main roles during the workshop. I started with basic algorithmic thinking and then connected those ideas to coding and a real robot.

01

Algorithm & Logic Instructor

I introduced the students to how computers follow instructions step by step, including the difference between inputs and outputs and the idea behind conditional statements.

The Programmer & Robot Game

We played "Programmer and Robot" game to help students understand algorithm easily. One student became the "Programmer" and another became the "Robot". The programmer had to give precise instructions while the robot followed them literally. If the instruction was unclear, the robot couldn't complete the task.

02

Scratch & Hardware Demonstrator

After introducing the logic, I demonstrated my 4WD ESP32-CAM rover. I drove it over Wi-Fi and showed the students the live camera feed.

From Logic to a Real Machine

We then used Scratch to work through the rover's basic control logic, including moving forward, turning, changing speed and stopping.

03 · HANDS-ON LEARNING

From Ideas to Circuits

We tried to keep the workshop practical. Instead of only explaining concepts, we moved from simple ideas to visual coding and finally to physical circuits.

01

Computing Basics

We talked about what computers are, basic hardware components, inputs and outputs, and how instructions are processed.

02

Scratch Coding

Students used visual blocks to explore movement and control logic and connect those ideas to the rover demonstration.

03

Arduino Traffic Light

Students wired red, yellow and green LEDs and used timing loops to create a simple traffic-light sequence.

04

Potentiometer LED Dimmer

Students used a potentiometer as an analog input and changed LED brightness using PWM as the output.

04 · REFLECTION

What I Learned From Teaching

I expected to be teaching the students, but I ended up learning quite a lot myself. Explaining something to someone who has never seen it before is very different from understanding it in your own head.

For example, if I couldn't explain what an analog pin or a loop was without using a lot of technical words, I knew I hadn't broken the idea down enough. The "Programmer & Robot" activity also reminded me why precise instructions matter in programming: a computer will not fill in the gaps for you.

The part I enjoyed most

Seeing the students become more confident was probably my favourite part. At the beginning, some of them were hesitant to touch the components. Once their first LED circuit worked, they became much more willing to try things themselves.

It also made me appreciate hands-on learning more. A concept can sound abstract when you explain it on a board, but it becomes much easier to understand when a student can actually see an LED turn on because of something they just built.

05 · WHY IT MATTERS TO ME

Why This Experience Fits My Engineering Interests

What I liked about the workshop was the combination of software and hardware. We could talk about an algorithm, write some code, connect a circuit and then see the result physically.

Programming Instructions, conditions, loops and Scratch logic
Electronics LEDs, potentiometers, breadboards and Arduino circuits
Robotics ESP32-CAM rover, motors, Wi-Fi control and live video

It was a small workshop, but it showed me exactly why I enjoy building things where software has a physical result.
